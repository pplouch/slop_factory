# Feature backlog 8: real coastlines, wave placement, Lake removal, mini-game VFX

_Historical record for [Rogue idle slop factory survivor rpg ultimate](../../CLAUDE.md) — what landed and why, kept for provenance, not needed for day-to-day work._

A follow-up ad hoc request after backlog 7's own water/biome pass: the coastlines it produced still looked "unorganic, squared" in a real screenshot; the shoreline foam had visible waves but open water didn't (the opposite of the ask); Lake (misremembered by the player as "water puddles", since the earlier removed feature actually was called that) needed removing entirely; resources could spawn floating in water; and Chest/SlotMachine were flagged as needing real animation/VFX instead of an instant text-swap.

- **Fixed the jagged/blocky coastlines**: root cause was resolution, not the noise itself — `Biomes.water_tint_at`/`shore_factor_at` were baked into per-*vertex* color at `Chunk.SUBDIVISIONS`' spacing (one sample every 2.5 world units), and wherever the underlying river/lake noise crossed its threshold within less than that spacing, the mesh's own coarse triangle grid faceted the boundary into a comb of squared teeth — confirmed visually in a real-renderer screenshot. Fixed by baking both into a dedicated, much finer (`Chunk.WATER_MASK_SIZE = 64`) *non-tiling* per-chunk texture pair instead (`_build_ground_material`'s second half): one texel every ~0.39 world units, sampled by `ground.gdshader` directly from the mesh's own local `VERTEX.xz` (not through the grain texture's `uv_scale`-tiled `UV`, since this mask needs to map exactly once onto one chunk) — a real-renderer screenshot at the same known water location now shows a smooth, organic winding shoreline with no visible chunk-grid snap. `Chunk._build_ground_mesh`'s per-vertex color now only carries `Biomes.blended_ground_color_at` (unaffected, its bands are wide enough that vertex resolution was never the problem).
- **Swapped wave placement (shore vs. deep water)**: the ask was "small flat waves that fit the coast shape... add waves farther from the coast" — the previous pass had it backwards (only the shoreline foam moved at all; the ground's own water tint elsewhere was static, and the separate Lake mesh with the real wave shader was small and rare). `Biomes.water_depth_factor_at` (new) is a monotonic 0 (right at the water's edge) → 1 (past `is_deep_water_at`'s own threshold) companion to `shore_factor_at`'s peaked-at-the-edge shape, baked into the same new mask texture's G channel. `ground.gdshader` now derives an analytic tangent-space wave normal (partial derivatives of two traveling sine waves, amplitude scaled by `depth`) and blends it into the same `NORMAL_MAP`/`NORMAL_MAP_DEPTH` slope the base grain texture already perturbs — a lighting-only fake bump rather than real vertex displacement, since `Chunk.SUBDIVISIONS`' vertex spacing isn't dense enough to resolve an actual wave shape without looking faceted (the same reasoning the base normal map already relies on). Shore foam amplitude was reduced to read as "small and flat" per the request, and now correctly follows the real per-pixel shore mask instead of the old aliased vertex-color one.
  - Hit and fixed a real bug in `water_depth_factor_at` mid-pass: `river_noise`/`lake_noise` are independent fields that can both classify a single point as water at once (e.g. a point deep inside a lake can coincidentally also fall within the river band purely by chance) — an early if/elif version checked river first and returned immediately, understating depth whenever the *other* field was actually the deep one at that point. Fixed by computing both fields' own depth contribution independently and taking the `max`, matching `is_deep_water_at`'s own OR-across-both-fields logic exactly. Caught by `test_water_depth_factor_at_is_zero_outside_water_and_saturates_deep_inside_it`'s grid scan (random sampling too easily misses rivers' narrow bands, the same reasoning `test_deep_water_implies_water_and_both_are_actually_findable` already used).
- **Merged overlapping rivers/lakes** (a follow-up report against this same pass, after a screenshot showed a visible seam: a lighter, differently-tinted winding ribbon cutting straight through an otherwise-uniform lake): the exact same class of bug as `water_depth_factor_at`'s own fix above, just still present in `water_tint_at`/`shore_factor_at` — both checked river's noise band first and returned whatever it said regardless of what the lake's own (unrelated) noise said at that point, so wherever a river's band happened to numerically graze a lake, the river's shallow/shore tint (or foam) won out over the lake's much deeper one purely by which `if` came first in the source. Fixed by consolidating all three functions (`water_tint_at`/`shore_factor_at`/`water_depth_factor_at`) into thin wrappers around one new shared `Biomes.water_sample_at`, which computes river's and lake's own full (tint, shore, depth) independently — each source's formulas already clamp to a neutral (white, 0, 0) far from their own feature, so no "is this source even active here" gating is needed — and blends between them by a normalized per-source "how confidently/deeply water" strength (`smoothstep` on the strength difference) rather than a hard switch, so a genuine river/lake confluence eases between the two instead of popping, and a coincidental graze lets whichever source is actually deeper here dominate smoothly. `Chunk._build_ground_material`'s water-mask bake loop was updated to call `water_sample_at` once per texel instead of the previous three separate `Biomes` calls, as a side-effect performance win (river/lake noise sampled once per texel instead of three times).
  - Loosened `test_water_depth_factor_at_is_zero_outside_water_and_saturates_deep_inside_it`'s deep-water tolerance from `0.0001` to `0.02` after the blend-based rewrite: a point that's genuinely deep in one source but also borderline-close in the *other* source's own band can now land a fraction of a percent under the old test's razor-tight 1.0 expectation — a deliberate, correct side effect of real blending (the whole point of this fix) rather than a regression.
- **Fixed a second, separate river/lake bug the first merge fix didn't catch**: a follow-up screenshot still showed a river's own tint band visible cutting across an otherwise-uniform lake, even after `water_sample_at` was already blending by whichever source's `strength` was higher. Root cause: `_river_water_sample`'s tint-saturation curve was calibrated against `RIVER_HALF_WIDTH * DEEP_RIVER_FRACTION` (reaching full `DEEP_WATER_TINT_COLOR` only very close to a river's exact center) while `_lake_water_sample`'s was calibrated against `DEEP_LAKE_MARGIN` (reaching full depth in lockstep with its own dominance `strength`) — two independently-shaped curves that happened to share a normalization denominator with the *dominance* comparison but not with each other's actual color output, so correctly picking "which source wins" didn't guarantee the two sources' tints actually *agreed* at that crossover point. Fixed by replacing each source's own independent tint formula with one shared `Biomes._tint_for_wetness(wetness)` curve, fed by a new per-source `wetness` value normalized onto the *same* 0..1(+) scale for both (0 = the shore band's outer dry edge, `SHORE_WETNESS` = right at the water line, 1.0 = the `is_deep_water_at` threshold, continuing to extrapolate past 1 toward that source's own visual center) — since both sources now funnel through the identical curve, wherever their `wetness` values coincide (exactly `water_sample_at`'s blend crossover condition) their tints already agree too, by construction, rather than needing two independently-tuned curves to happen to match. Verified with a fresh screenshot at the same location: the lake now reads as one uniform tone with no visible seam anywhere in the open water.
  - Added `test_tint_for_wetness_endpoints_match_dry_land_and_full_depth`, `test_tint_for_wetness_is_continuous_within_the_shore_and_water_zones`, and `test_river_and_lake_samples_route_their_tint_through_the_shared_wetness_curve` (the last one a direct regression guard against either source's tint drifting back to an independently-shaped curve). The continuity test deliberately does **not** assert continuity *across* the shore/water boundary itself (`SHORE_WETNESS`) — `_tint_for_wetness` keeps a crisp sand-to-water color change there, same as the pre-existing (untouched, not part of this bug) design before either source was routed through a shared curve; a real beach line reading as a visible material edge is expected, not the bug this pass fixed (river and lake *disagreeing with each other out in open water*, not the shore having a visible edge at all).
- **Investigated, and ruled out as an actual code bug**: the same follow-up report also flagged thin light-green grid lines crossing the terrain exactly at chunk borders. Traced to Godot's own "Visible Collision Shapes" editor debug-draw feature rendering the outline of each chunk's flat `StaticBody3D`/`BoxShape3D` (tiled edge-to-edge at exactly `Chunk.CHUNK_SIZE` spacing, see `Chunk._build_ground`) — confirmed nothing in `scripts/` draws anything at 25-unit spacing in that color, `project.godot` has no matching debug-shapes setting, and `DebugOverlayManager`'s own grid overlay (a genuine in-game debug feature) defaults off, is a different color (white, not green), and is spaced at `BuildingManager.GRID_CELL_SIZE` (2.0 units) rather than 25. This is a per-session Godot **editor** preference (Debug menu → "Visible Collision Shapes"), external to the project's own files, not something a code change can fix — toggling it off in the editor is the actual resolution, and it never appears in an exported/played build regardless.
- **Removed Lake entirely** (see feature request: "what I called 'water puddles' are in fact 'lake' in the code, they are not needed, remove them"): `lake.tscn` deleted, `Chunk._maybe_spawn_water`/`LAKE_SCENE`/`WATER_PLACEMENT_ATTEMPTS` all removed. Checked first whether anything actually depended on a Lake *node* existing (not just the ground's own `is_water_at` terrain classification) before removing it: Lake was the sole scene ever setting `resource_type = "water"`, making it the only hand-harvestable water source (`OrderManager.ITEM_REQUIRED_FOR_RESOURCE` gates it behind a "bucket" tool) — but `WaterExtractor` is a documented stub with zero production logic ("not yet functional -- placement only"), `WaterTank` only stores/relays whatever `resource_type == "water"` items arrive without producing any itself, and neither building's placement validity depends on a Lake node (both gate on `Biomes.is_water_at` terrain classification directly). Grepping the full `scripts/` tree also confirmed no building cost/recipe anywhere requires "water" as an input. Net effect: removing Lake makes "water" resource gathering vestigial (no producer left) rather than actually breaking anything currently functional — `WaterExtractor`/`WaterTank`/the bucket-tool gate are left in place as-is (harmless, and plausibly the seed of a future real water-extraction feature) rather than also ripped out, since removing them wasn't asked for.
- **Resources no longer spawn on water** (see feature request: "resources cannot be placed on water"): `Chunk._scatter_resources` was the one spawn function in the file with no water check at all — `_maybe_spawn_chest`/`_maybe_spawn_village`/`_maybe_spawn_slot_machine` (and `PropScatter`'s own decorative foliage) already skip-if-water, confirmed by reading each. Fixed by adding the same `Biomes.is_water_at` skip-without-retry check per candidate point, so a biome bordering a lot of water just ends up with a slightly thinner resource cluster instead of a tree floating in a river.
- **Chest VFX/animation** (see feature request: "improve Chest UI... filled with VFX and animation, like a real slot machine"): `Chest.open()` still grants its whole loot table and disables further interaction (`remove_from_group("chests")`, `collision_layer = 0`) the instant it's called — the economy never waits on an animation — but now plays a lid-opening tween (`Tween.TRANS_BACK` swing + lift), a bigger gold `Effects.spawn_impact` burst, and a glow-light pulse before actually `queue_free`-ing itself, all via `await`s inside the otherwise-`void` function (a fire-and-forget async call, same pattern Godot signal handlers already use). `ChestPanel` replaced its single static multi-line contents `Label` with individually color-coded (`Effects.resource_color`), staggered fade-in loot lines, and lingers briefly on a flashed "Claimed!" button state before closing instead of an instant menu-close.
- **SlotMachine VFX/animation**: `SlotMachine.spin()` still resolves the RNG roll, payout, and economy transaction synchronously in one call (no exploit window from delaying that) and gained an `is_jackpot` flag on its return dict, plus its own in-world flourish — a lever-pull tween (fired on every spin, win or lose, so the machine always visibly reacts to being played) and a screen-glow pulse scaled bigger/longer for a jackpot than an ordinary win. The actual reel-spin *reveal* animation lives entirely in `SlotMachinePanel` instead of the machine script: 3 separate reel `Label`s (replacing the old single "Wood | Gold | Jackpot" text line) cycle through random symbol names at a fast constant rate and lock onto the machine's real, already-decided result one at a time with a staggered stop + landing bounce (left to right, like a real machine's reels winding down) before the win/lose message pops in — a gold "JACKPOT!" callout distinct from an ordinary win.
- Added test coverage for the new `Biomes.water_depth_factor_at` (bounds, near-origin, and the grid-scan saturation check that caught the max-vs-early-return bug above), plus new `tests/world_objects/chest_test.gd`/`slot_machine_test.gd` covering `Chest.open()`'s immediate (pre-animation) resource grant/group-removal/collision-disable, and `SlotMachine.spin()`'s afford-ability gating, cost-spent bounds, and `is_jackpot` flag wiring against `_evaluate` (already documented as designed for exactly this kind of direct pure-function test).
- Verified end to end: headless import/smoke checks clean, the full gdUnit4 suite (111 test cases after this pass) all passing (stable across repeated runs, since several new tests depend on live RNG rather than a fixed seed), a full runtime exercise of the actual `ChestPanel`/`SlotMachinePanel` flow (not just the underlying scripts) via a temporary debug hook confirming no errors from the new tweens/awaits/node-path references, and a real-renderer screenshot at a known water location confirming the coastline now reads as smooth and organic instead of the old comb-toothed edge.
